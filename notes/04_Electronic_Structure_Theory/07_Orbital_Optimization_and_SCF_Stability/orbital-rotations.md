## Why parametrize orbitals by an exponential

Orbital optimization (Hartree-Fock, MCSCF) needs a way to vary the orbitals that automatically keeps them orthonormal. Varying MO coefficients directly needs constraints (Lagrange multipliers, re-orthonormalization). Writing every allowed change as a unitary transformation $\mathbf U = e^{\boldsymbol\kappa}$ with unconstrained parameters $\kappa_{pq}$ removes the constraints. In second quantization the same transformation acts on the wave function as an operator, $e^{\hat\kappa}$.

## The generator

**Jørgensen-Simons form.** J-S write the transformation as $e^{i\hat\lambda}$ with

$$
\hat\lambda = \sum_{rs}\lambda_{rs}\,a_r^\dagger a_s .
$$

**Transformed creation operators.** Conjugate a creation operator:

$$
\tilde a_t^\dagger \equiv e^{i\hat\lambda}a_t^\dagger e^{-i\hat\lambda} = a_t^\dagger + [i\hat\lambda, a_t^\dagger] + \tfrac1{2!}[i\hat\lambda,[i\hat\lambda, a_t^\dagger]] + \cdots
$$

The basic commutator lowers the operator rank: $[a_r^\dagger a_s, a_t^\dagger] = \delta_{st}a_r^\dagger$, so $[\hat\lambda, a_t^\dagger] = \sum_r\lambda_{rt}a_r^\dagger$ and $[\hat\lambda,[\hat\lambda, a_t^\dagger]] = \sum_r(\boldsymbol\lambda^2)_{rt}a_r^\dagger$. Summing the series (the nested-commutator expansion of [[baker-campbell-hausdorff]]),

$$
\tilde a_t^\dagger = \sum_r a_r^\dagger\,\big[e^{i\boldsymbol\lambda}\big]_{rt} .
$$

Each nested commutator is again a single creation operator; the series in operators becomes a series in matrices.

**Action on a determinant.** Because $\hat\lambda|\text{vac}\rangle = 0$, inserting $e^{-i\hat\lambda}e^{i\hat\lambda} = 1$ between the creation operators gives

$$
e^{i\hat\lambda}\,a_1^\dagger a_2^\dagger\cdots a_N^\dagger|\text{vac}\rangle = \tilde a_1^\dagger\tilde a_2^\dagger\cdots\tilde a_N^\dagger|\text{vac}\rangle .
$$

The determinant of the original orbitals is turned into the determinant of the transformed orbitals $\tilde\phi_t = \sum_r\phi_r[e^{i\boldsymbol\lambda}]_{rt}$. By linearity the same holds for any CI expansion in that orbital set: the CI coefficients stay, the orbitals change.

## Unitarity and the real case

**Condition.** The transformed operators obey the anticommutation relations $[\tilde a_t^\dagger, \tilde a_s]_+ = \delta_{ts}$ provided $\hat\lambda$ is Hermitian, $\lambda_{rs} = \lambda_{sr}^*$. Then $e^{i\boldsymbol\lambda}$ is a unitary matrix and orthonormality is preserved.

**Splitting.** Write $\boldsymbol\lambda = \boldsymbol\alpha + i\boldsymbol\kappa'$ with $\boldsymbol\alpha$ real symmetric and $\boldsymbol\kappa'$ real antisymmetric. Then

$$
\hat\lambda = \sum_r\alpha_{rr}a_r^\dagger a_r + \sum_{r>s}\alpha_{rs}(a_r^\dagger a_s + a_s^\dagger a_r) + i\sum_{r>s}\kappa'_{rs}(a_r^\dagger a_s - a_s^\dagger a_r).
$$

The $\boldsymbol\alpha$ part makes real orbitals complex. For real orbitals it is dropped, and the transformation becomes a real orthogonal one generated by a real antisymmetric matrix.

**Convention used in this knowledge base.** Write the real rotation as

$$
\hat\kappa = \sum_{pq}\kappa_{pq}a_p^\dagger a_q = \sum_{p>q}\kappa_{pq}\big(a_p^\dagger a_q - a_q^\dagger a_p\big),\qquad \kappa_{pq} = -\kappa_{qp},
$$

$$
e^{\hat\kappa}a_q^\dagger e^{-\hat\kappa} = \sum_p a_p^\dagger\,[e^{\boldsymbol\kappa}]_{pq},
\qquad \tilde\phi_q = \sum_p\phi_p[e^{\boldsymbol\kappa}]_{pq},\qquad \mathbf C\to\mathbf C\,e^{\boldsymbol\kappa}.
$$

This is J-S's $e^{i\hat\lambda}$ with $i\boldsymbol\lambda = \boldsymbol\kappa$ (their text writes it as $\exp(-\kappa)$ with the opposite sign convention for $\kappa$; only the sign of the parameters differs).

## Evaluating the matrix exponential

**Diagonalization (J-S Eqs. 1.55-1.57).** If $\mathbf u$ diagonalizes $\boldsymbol\lambda$, $\boldsymbol\lambda = \mathbf u\,\mathbf d\,\mathbf u^\dagger$, then $e^{i\boldsymbol\lambda} = \mathbf u\,e^{i\mathbf d}\mathbf u^\dagger$ ([[functions-of-matrices]]). For a real antisymmetric $\boldsymbol\kappa$ the eigenvalues are $\pm i\omega_k$ (and zeros); equivalently diagonalize the Hermitian $i\boldsymbol\kappa$ or $-\boldsymbol\kappa^2$.

**Occupied-virtual rotations only (general knowledge, not from J-S).** If only the block $\kappa_{ai}$ (virtual $a$, occupied $i$) is nonzero, with $\boldsymbol\kappa = \begin{pmatrix}\mathbf 0 & -\mathbf X^{T}\\ \mathbf X & \mathbf 0\end{pmatrix}$ and the SVD $\mathbf X = \mathbf V\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf W^T$, then

$$
e^{\boldsymbol\kappa} = \begin{pmatrix}\mathbf 1 + \mathbf W(\cos\boldsymbol\Sigma - \mathbf 1)\mathbf W^T & -\mathbf W\sin\boldsymbol\Sigma\,\mathbf V^T\\ \mathbf V\sin\boldsymbol\Sigma\,\mathbf W^T & \mathbf 1 + \mathbf V(\cos\boldsymbol\Sigma - \mathbf 1)\mathbf V^T\end{pmatrix}
$$

(thin SVD; occupied block first). This is a set of independent $2\times2$ rotations by angles $\sigma_k$ between paired occupied and virtual orbitals. Checked against `scipy.linalg.expm` for a random $5\times3$ block: difference $9\times10^{-16}$.

**Two orbitals.** $\boldsymbol\kappa = \begin{pmatrix}0 & -\theta\\ \theta & 0\end{pmatrix}$ gives $e^{\boldsymbol\kappa} = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta & -\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta\end{pmatrix}$, the one-parameter rotation used for H$_2$ in [[h2-dissociation-rhf-uhf]].

## Redundant rotations

**Inside a determinant.** Rotating occupied orbitals among themselves multiplies a single determinant by $\det(\mathbf U_{\text{occ}})$, a phase; for a proper rotation the determinant is unchanged. The same holds for virtual-virtual rotations. Only the $o\times v$ occupied-virtual parameters change a Hartree-Fock wave function.

**Inside an MCSCF wave function.** Whether a rotation is redundant depends on the configuration space: in a complete active space the active-active rotations can be absorbed into the CI coefficients and are redundant ([[mcscf-second-order-optimization]]). Redundant parameters make the Hessian singular and are removed before optimization.

**Connection to Thouless's theorem (general knowledge).** Any determinant not orthogonal to $|\Psi_0\rangle$ can be written $e^{\hat T_1}|\Psi_0\rangle$ up to normalization, with $\hat T_1$ a pure occupied-to-virtual excitation operator; $e^{\hat\kappa}$ with the de-excitation part included is the unitary version. This is why singles in coupled cluster act as orbital relaxation ([[one-particle-hamiltonian-models]], [[coupled-cluster-second-quantization]]).

!!! example "Numerical checks with Fock-space matrices (computed for this note)"
    Four spin orbitals of one spin, operators built by the Jordan-Wigner construction, random real antisymmetric $\boldsymbol\kappa$:

    - $e^{\hat\kappa}a_0^\dagger e^{-\hat\kappa} - \sum_p a_p^\dagger[e^{\boldsymbol\kappa}]_{p0}$: largest element $2\times10^{-14}$.
    - $e^{\hat\kappa}a_0^\dagger a_1^\dagger|\text{vac}\rangle$ against the determinant of the two rotated orbitals: difference $3\times10^{-14}$.
    - A pure occupied-occupied rotation ($\kappa_{01} = 0.7$) leaves $a_0^\dagger a_1^\dagger|\text{vac}\rangle$ unchanged exactly.

```python
from scipy.linalg import expm
khat = sum(k[p, q] * ad[p] @ a[q] for p in range(n) for q in range(n))   # Fock-space generator
U = expm(k)                                                               # orbital rotation matrix
lhs = expm(khat) @ ad[0] @ expm(-khat)
rhs = sum(U[p, 0] * ad[p] for p in range(n))                              # rotated creation operator
assert abs(lhs - rhs).max() < 1e-12
```

## Helgaker, Jørgensen and Olsen's formulation and symmetry-restricted rotations

**Sign convention.** HJO write the rotated orbitals as $\tilde{\boldsymbol\phi} = \boldsymbol\phi\,\exp(-\boldsymbol\kappa)$ with anti-Hermitian $\boldsymbol\kappa$, and

$$
\tilde a_P^\dagger = \exp(-\hat\kappa)\,a_P^\dagger\exp(\hat\kappa) = \sum_Qa_Q^\dagger[\exp(-\boldsymbol\kappa)]_{QP},\qquad
|\tilde0\rangle = \exp(-\hat\kappa)|0\rangle,\qquad \hat\kappa = \sum_{PQ}\kappa_{PQ}a_P^\dagger a_Q .
$$

This is the convention above with $\boldsymbol\kappa\to-\boldsymbol\kappa$. HJO's ordering ("the minus sign in the exponential is conventional") is the one used in their later chapters on Hartree-Fock, MCSCF and coupled cluster. The proof is the one given above: each nested commutator of $\hat\kappa$ with a creator is again a single creator, and $\hat\kappa|\text{vac}\rangle = 0$. The exponential parametrization of the unitary matrices themselves is in [[unitary-matrices-exponential-logarithm]].

**Why restrict.** If $|0\rangle$ has definite spin and spatial symmetry and the optimized state must keep it, $\hat\kappa$ may contain only excitation operators that transform as the totally symmetric representation of the Hamiltonian. Otherwise $\hat\kappa|0\rangle$ mixes in other symmetries. A perturbation changes what is totally symmetric. HJO's example: O₂ in a field perpendicular to the bond allows only rotations that are totally symmetric in $C_{2v}$. Three common cases:

1. **Real versus imaginary rotations.** Field-free real wave functions need only real rotations. A magnetic field requires imaginary ones.
2. **Spin.** Only singlet rotations conserve spin. Spin perturbations need triplet rotations as well.
3. **Point group.** Only totally symmetric rotations preserve spatial symmetry, unless the geometry is distorted.

A further benefit is fewer parameters.

**Parameter counts.**

| Rotation operator | Real parameters ($M = 2n$ spin orbitals, $n$ orbitals) |
|---|---|
| General anti-Hermitian $\hat\kappa$ over spin orbitals | $M + 2\cdot\tfrac{M^2 - M}2 = M^2 = 4n^2$ |
| Same, in singlet and triplet tensor form (real and imaginary parts) | $4n^2$, now sorted by spin rank and reality |
| Real spin-orbital rotations, $\sum_{P>Q}\kappa_{PQ}(a_P^\dagger a_Q - a_Q^\dagger a_P)$ | $M(M-1)/2$ |
| Real singlet rotations, $\sum_{p>q}\kappa_{pq}E_{pq}^-$, $E_{pq}^- = E_{pq} - E_{qp}$ | $n(n-1)/2$ |

**Real orbital optimization.** For real, spin-adapted wave functions only the last line is needed. This is the rotation operator of RHF, closed-shell MCSCF and CASSCF. The triplet and imaginary parts are kept for response to spin-dependent or magnetic perturbations ([[scf-second-order-and-stability]], [[molecular-properties-and-response]]).

!!! example "Checks in a 64-dimensional Fock space (3 orbitals; computed for this note)"
    - **Rotated creators.** $\exp(-\hat\kappa)a_{p\sigma}^\dagger\exp(\hat\kappa) = \sum_qa_{q\sigma}^\dagger[\exp(-\boldsymbol\kappa)]_{qp}$ for a random real antisymmetric $\boldsymbol\kappa$ in the singlet operator $\hat\kappa = \sum_{pq}\kappa_{pq}E_{pq}$: deviation $4\times10^{-14}$.
    - **Spin conservation.** Start from the open-shell singlet CSF $(a_{0\alpha}^\dagger a_{1\beta}^\dagger - a_{0\beta}^\dagger a_{1\alpha}^\dagger)|\text{vac}\rangle/\sqrt2$, with $\langle\hat S^2\rangle = 0$.
        - A singlet rotation gives $\langle\hat S^2\rangle = 0.000000$.
        - The same parameters in a triplet rotation built from $\sqrt2\hat T_{pq}^{1,0}$ give $\langle\hat S^2\rangle = 0.3534$, with $\langle\hat S_z\rangle$ still 0. The triplet rotation breaks $S$ but not $M$.

## Two orbitals in a nonorthogonal basis (J-S Problem 1.5)

**Setup.** Two orthonormal orbitals $\phi_{1,2} = a_{1,2}\chi_a + b_{1,2}\chi_b$ in a basis $\chi_a,\chi_b$ that need not be orthonormal. The single rotation parameter gives

$$
e^{-\boldsymbol\kappa} = \cos\kappa\begin{pmatrix}1 & 0\\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} + \sin\kappa\begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\ -1 & 0\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\kappa & \sin\kappa\\ -\sin\kappa & \cos\kappa\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \boldsymbol\kappa = \begin{pmatrix}0 & -\kappa\\ \kappa & 0\end{pmatrix},
$$

from summing the exponential series with $\boldsymbol\kappa^2 = -\kappa^2\mathbf 1$ ([[baker-campbell-hausdorff]]).

**Applying it.** The rotated orbitals are $(\tilde\phi_1\ \tilde\phi_2) = (\chi_a\ \chi_b)\begin{pmatrix}a_1 & a_2\\ b_1 & b_2\end{pmatrix}e^{-\boldsymbol\kappa}$. For $\kappa = 10^\circ = 0.174$ rad:

$$
\tilde\phi_1 = (0.985a_1 - 0.174a_2)\chi_a + (0.985b_1 - 0.174b_2)\chi_b,\qquad
\tilde\phi_2 = (0.174a_1 + 0.985a_2)\chi_a + (0.174b_1 + 0.985b_2)\chi_b .
$$

**Basis overlap does not matter.** Orthonormality is preserved automatically, because the rotation acts on the orthonormal MOs, not on the basis functions. J-S use exactly this step for HeH⁺ in their second-order SCF and MCSCF problems ([[heh-plus-minimal-basis-model]]).
